Section 01

Executive Summary

What we found, why it matters, and what it is costing you.

The short version: The Roof Xperts has the two things that are hardest to build — a licensed, 30-year operating history (CGC1536478 / CCC1331579) and genuinely excellent review scores (4.9 Angi, 4.9 HomeAdvisor, 5.0 Yelp, BBB-accredited since 2019). What it does not have is a web presence capable of converting any of that into search rankings.

The brand is currently running two separate websites, is listed at at least four different street addresses across the directories Google uses to validate a local business, and has no dedicated page for any of the services it sells or any of the fifteen cities it claims to serve. In a market where competitors rank on purpose-built city and service pages, that is the difference between appearing in the map pack and being invisible.

The single most expensive problem

A second live website — trx.company, branded "The Roof Xperts Co" — is indexed by Google alongside theroofxperts.com. It carries a different email address, a different page structure, and unfinished WordPress defaults visible in public search results. Every link, review mention, and brand search is being split across two domains, so neither one accumulates enough authority to rank. Consolidating these is the fastest single win available.

Section 03 — Part A

Critical Findings

Five issues that are actively preventing the site from ranking. Fix these first.

C-01

Two live websites are competing for the same brand

Critical
EvidenceBoth theroofxperts.com and trx.company ("The Roof Xperts Co") are indexed by Google. trx.company carries a full page set — /services/, /roof-repair/, /residential-roofing/, /emergency-roofing/, /projects/, /process/, /about-us/, /contact/ — and a different contact email (theroofxpertsco@gmail.com vs Info@TheRoofXperts.com).
Why it hurtsGoogle has to pick one domain to associate with "The Roof Xperts." Inbound links, citations, brand searches, and review mentions are split across both, so neither accumulates enough authority to compete. Worse, the two sites cannibalize each other for the same keywords — a self-inflicted duplicate-content problem.
The fixPick one canonical domain (theroofxperts.com — it matches the brand, the email, and every directory citation). Map every trx.company URL to its best equivalent and 301-redirect it. Migrate any content worth keeping first. This consolidates all accumulated authority into one asset.
TimelineWeek 1–2. Ranking response typically visible in 30–60 days.
C-02

Unfinished WordPress defaults are publicly indexed

Critical
EvidenceGoogle is displaying the title tag "Contact – My WordPress - The Roof Xperts Co" and "Emergency Roofing Services - My WordPress" for trx.company pages — the WordPress installation's default site title was never changed. The default /sample-page/ stub is also live and indexed.
Why it hurtsTwo problems. To Google, default placeholder content and orphaned stub pages are a quality signal — they indicate an unmaintained site, and unmaintained sites do not get ranked for commercial queries. To a homeowner about to spend $12,000, seeing "My WordPress" in a search result for their roofing contractor is a credibility failure at the worst possible moment.
The fixResolved by C-01. Once trx.company is redirected, these URLs stop existing. If any part of that site is retained short-term, the site title, all title tags, and the sample page need immediate correction and a re-crawl request.
TimelineWeek 1. Same-day fix if trx.company must stay live temporarily.
C-03

At least four conflicting business addresses across the web

Critical
Evidence 1000 S Dixie Hwy W #8, Pompano Beach FL 33060 (own website) · 4699 N Federal Hwy, Pompano Beach FL (Yelp) · 2091 NE 36th St #5208, Lighthouse Point FL (BBB) · 2071 NE 1st Ave, Pompano Beach FL 33060-4901 (ZoomInfo / Dun & Bradstreet) · plus a HomeAdvisor listing showing ZIP 33066. Hours also conflict: Mon–Fri 9–5 on Yahoo Local and HomeAdvisor vs Mon–Fri 8–5 on Yelp.
Why it hurtsNAP consistency is one of the strongest ranking factors in Google's local algorithm. When Google finds five versions of a business's address, it lowers confidence in all of them — which directly suppresses map-pack placement. For a home-services business, the map pack is the lead source. This alone can be the difference between three-pack placement and page two.
The fixEstablish one canonical NAP record. Audit and correct the top 40–60 citation sources, in priority order: Google Business Profile, Apple Business Connect, Bing Places, BBB, Yelp, Angi, HomeAdvisor, Facebook, Yahoo, then aggregators (Data Axle, Foursquare, Neustar). Publish the canonical NAP on-site in structured data and in the footer of every page.
TimelineWeek 2–6. Aggregator propagation runs 60–90 days.
C-04

Duplicate directory profiles are splitting reviews and confusing Google

Critical
EvidenceTwo separate HomeAdvisor profiles exist (ID 73845684 and ID 109964797, the latter under ZIP 33066). BBB carries the business under both a Lighthouse Point and a Pompano Beach profile URL. Yelp lists a third address.
Why it hurtsDuplicate listings divide review counts, so a business with genuinely strong ratings looks thinner than it is on every platform. Duplicates are also the single most common cause of Google Business Profile suspension — a risk that turns an SEO problem into a total loss of map visibility overnight.
The fixClaim every profile, merge or request removal of duplicates, and consolidate reviews onto the surviving listing where the platform allows it. Then lock the canonical record in.
TimelineWeek 2–8. Platform merge requests are the long pole.
C-05

No service pages and no location pages on the primary domain

Critical
EvidenceIndexed pages found on theroofxperts.com are limited to the homepage, /about-us, /financing, and /project-photo-gallery. There is no page for roof replacement, roof repair, tile roofing, metal roofing, flat/TPO commercial roofing, roof inspections, or storm and insurance-claim work — and no page for any of the fifteen cities in the stated service area. The service pages that do exist are on the wrong domain (trx.company).
Why it hurtsGoogle ranks pages, not businesses. A search for "roof replacement Fort Lauderdale" needs a page about roof replacement in Fort Lauderdale to rank for it. With four thin pages, the site is eligible for essentially one query — its own brand name — which only captures people who already know the company. Every competitor named in this report runs a service × location page matrix. That is the entire game in this vertical.
The fixBuild the matrix: 8–10 service pages × 8–15 city pages, each with genuinely local content (permit and HVHZ code specifics, local project photos, neighborhood references, city-specific testimonials), properly interlinked and schema-marked. Not doorway pages — substantive pages that earn the ranking.
TimelineWeek 3–16, released in waves. This is the primary traffic driver.
Section 03 — Part B

High-Priority Findings

Issues that cap the ceiling on how much traffic the site can earn.

H-01

Title tags are duplicated, non-descriptive, and missing geography

High
EvidenceHomepage title is "The Roof Xperts" — brand only, no service, no city. /financing also titles as "THE ROOF XPERTS", a duplicate carrying no information about the page. /about-us titles as "Best Roofing Company Near Me - THE ROOF XPERTS" — a generic keyword phrase stuffed onto the one page that should be building trust, not chasing traffic. /project-photo-gallery titles as "Project Photo Gallery" with no brand and no geography at all.
Why it hurtsThe title tag is still the single highest-leverage on-page ranking element and the primary driver of click-through rate from search results. Duplicates force Google to choose between pages. A homepage title with no service and no city cannot rank for a service-and-city query.
The fixA consistent title formula across the site: Primary Service in City | Differentiator | Brand, each unique, each under ~60 characters. Example homepage: "Roof Replacement & Repair in Pompano Beach, FL | Licensed 30+ Yrs | The Roof Xperts". Same treatment for meta descriptions, written as ad copy with financing and free-estimate offers.
H-02

No content engine — zero topical authority

High
EvidenceNo blog, resource section, cost guide, FAQ hub, or case-study library found on either domain.
Why it hurtsRoofing buyers research for weeks before they call. The queries they run — "how much does a roof cost in Broward County," "will insurance cover my roof," "how long does a tile roof last in Florida," "what is HVHZ certification" — are where the relationship starts. Competitors are capturing that entire research phase. You are not in it. Topical content is also how a domain earns the authority to rank its commercial pages.
The fixA South Florida-specific content program: roof cost guides by material and city, the insurance-claim process in Florida, hurricane preparation and post-storm inspection, HVHZ and Florida Building Code explainers, material comparison guides. Each one internally links to the matching service and city page.
H-03

Credentials and experience are not surfaced where Google reads them

High
EvidenceLicenses CGC1536478 and CCC1331579 and the 30-year operating history appear in third-party directories and on Instagram, but do not appear in any indexed title tag, meta description, or search snippet for the website. No manufacturer certification (GAF, Owens Corning, CertainTeed), workmanship warranty, or insurance verification found in indexed content.
Why it hurtsRoofing is a Your-Money-Your-Life category — Google applies elevated experience, expertise, authoritativeness, and trust standards to it. Meanwhile the top-ranking competitors lead with exactly these signals: "GAF Platinum Elite," "HVHZ-certified," "employee-owned since 1983." You hold real credentials and are not getting a single point of credit for them.
The fixSurface licenses in the site footer, on every service page, and in structured data. Build a dedicated credentials/warranty page. Pursue manufacturer certification if not already held — it is a ranking signal, a trust signal, and a margin signal simultaneously.
H-04

Excellent reviews, fragmented and stranded off-site

High
Evidence4.9 on Angi · 4.9 on HomeAdvisor · 5.0 on Yelp · BBB-accredited since 2019 — spread across at least seven platforms including two duplicate HomeAdvisor profiles. No consolidated review display or Review schema found on the website. Google review volume could not be confirmed and appears thin relative to competitors.
Why it hurtsGoogle review count and velocity are among the strongest map-pack ranking factors, and they are the first thing a homeowner checks. Reviews scattered across seven platforms with no on-site aggregation means the social proof never reaches the person on your website, and never reaches the algorithm ranking your map listing.
The fixInstall an automated review-request system (SMS and email, triggered on job completion) pointed primarily at Google. Aggregate and display reviews on-site with Review and AggregateRating schema. Target a steady, sustainable cadence rather than a burst — velocity matters more than a one-time spike.
H-05

No structured data — ineligible for rich results

High
EvidenceNo schema-driven rich results (star ratings, FAQ expansions, breadcrumbs, business panels) observed in any search result for either domain.
Why it hurtsSchema is how you tell Google explicitly what the business is, where it operates, what it charges, and how it is rated — rather than hoping the crawler infers it. It also earns visual real estate in search results that measurably lifts click-through rate against competitors on the same page.
The fixFull implementation: RoofingContractor / LocalBusiness with canonical NAP, geo coordinates, hours, and service area; Service on every service page; FAQPage; Review and AggregateRating; BreadcrumbList; ImageObject on gallery projects.
H-06

Thin brand and social signal

High
EvidenceInstagram @theroofxperts: 213 followers, 87 posts. No evident content pipeline feeding the website.
Why it hurtsRoofing is one of the most visually persuasive trades there is — before/after work sells itself. Low brand search volume and a thin social footprint also weaken the brand-authority signals Google uses as a tiebreaker between similarly optimized local competitors.
The fixSystematize job-site capture (every crew, every job, same shot list), then run one pipeline into Instagram, Google Business Profile photo posts, and on-site project case studies simultaneously. One capture, three placements.
Section 04

Medium-Priority & Conversion Findings

Not ranking blockers — but each one is money left on the table.

M-01

Palm Beach phone number on a Broward-marketed business

Medium
EvidenceListed number is (561) 740-5516. The 561 area code covers Palm Beach County; the business address and the bulk of the claimed service area are Broward County (954).
Why it hurtsSouth Florida homeowners read area codes as a proxy for "are you actually local to me." A Pompano Beach or Fort Lauderdale customer seeing a 561 number reads it as an out-of-area contractor, which costs calls at the exact moment of decision.
The fixAdd a 954 tracking number as the primary display number in Broward-targeted content and the Google Business Profile, keeping 561 for Palm Beach targeting. Route both to the same line. This doubles as call-attribution data, so you can finally see which pages and which cities actually produce phone calls.
M-02

100% financing — your strongest offer — is buried

Medium
Evidence100% roof financing is offered, but appears only on a standalone /financing page whose title tag does not even mention financing.
Why it hurtsCost is the number one objection in a $10,000–$16,000 purchase. "No money down, monthly payments" removes it. An offer that strong belongs in the hero of every page, in every meta description, and in the Google Business Profile — not on a page nobody navigates to.
The fixElevate financing into the homepage hero, every service-page CTA, and meta descriptions site-wide. Add a payment calculator — high-engagement, highly linkable, and it pre-qualifies leads before your estimator spends a drive on them.
M-03

Google Business Profile appears unoptimized

Medium
EvidenceNo GBP-driven rich signals surfaced in search results; NAP conflicts (C-03) and duplicate listings (C-04) indicate the profile is not actively managed.
Why it hurtsFor a local roofer, the Google Business Profile typically drives more inbound calls than the website itself. An unmanaged profile — no service list, no products, no weekly posts, no Q&A seeding, no geotagged photo cadence, no review responses — forfeits the highest-intent traffic available.
The fixFull optimization pass, then an ongoing cadence: weekly posts, monthly geotagged project photos, complete service and product listings, seeded Q&A, and a response to every review within 48 hours.
M-04

No storm-damage or insurance-claim content

Medium
EvidenceNo indexed content addressing hurricane damage, emergency tarping, or the Florida roof insurance-claim process on the primary domain.
Why it hurtsIn South Florida this is the highest-intent, highest-margin traffic that exists, and it spikes hard and fast after every named storm. Contractors who have the pages already indexed capture it; contractors who start writing after landfall do not. Being unprepared here costs an entire season.
The fixBuild an evergreen storm hub now — emergency tarping, post-storm inspection checklists, the claim process step by step, working with adjusters, deductible and Florida statute explainers — so it is aged and indexed before the next event, with pre-built landing pages ready to activate.
M-05

Photo gallery is not working as an SEO asset

Medium
EvidenceA /project-photo-gallery page exists but appears to be a single undifferentiated page rather than individual, indexable project pages.
Why it hurtsCompleted jobs are the best long-tail ranking asset a contractor owns. "Tile roof replacement in Coral Springs" as its own case study — with the city, the material, the problem, the solution, the timeline, and the customer's own words — ranks for queries no competitor is even targeting, and converts far better than a photo grid.
The fixConvert the gallery into individual project pages, each tagged by city and material, each with ImageObject schema, each internally linked to the matching service and city page. Ten jobs a month becomes 120 new ranking assets a year.

Setting the expectation honestly

Local SEO is not a 30-day business. Realistic timeline for a domain in this condition:

  • Days 1–45: technical and citation fixes land. Map-pack position and impression volume usually move first — often meaningfully, because C-03 and C-04 are actively suppressing you right now.

  • Days 120–270: content and links compound. This is where the program starts clearly paying for itself.
  • Day 270+: defensible position. Competitors have to spend serious money to displace you.

Anyone promising page-one rankings in 30 days is selling you something they cannot deliver. The compounding is real, but it takes the time it takes.
